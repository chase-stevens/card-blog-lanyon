We talk a lot about cards here. But what exactly is a card?

Different card games will have different attributes, layouts, and data. However, across different card games, several common attributes arise.

#### Border
Starting from the outside in, we make note of the border of the card. The border is often invisible and unobserved. The contents of the card often grab the player's attention. However, several games make use of the border to indicate game rules, such as silver border cards in Magic the Gathering indicating an un-card that is not legal in most formats.

Some card games have card variants that remove the border. This is usually done with alternate art. The lack of border can be used to make the card feel larger and to highlight exceptional art.

#### Frame
We work inwards from the border and come to the frame of the card. The frame can be used to great effect to quickly tell players what kind of card they are looking at.

Pop Quiz: How many frames does Magic the Gathering have?

I wrote this question, then went to find the answer. Turns out that there are a lot. To help put it into context, the first set of Magic the Gathering had seven different frames - one frame for each of the five colors of mana, one for artifacts, and one for lands. Since then, they've updated their basic frames, tried new ones in a set called Future Sight, made frames for specific mechanics (then multiplied by five for each color), made new frames for new card types, and so on and so forth.

If you know the answer to the question, let me know at chasestevenspersonal+bricks@gmail.com.

Frames are made early and often. Hearthstone, Blizzard's digital card game, shipped with frames for each class, as well as neutral cards. A dragon sat around the outside of the art to indicate legendary cards as well -- a clever modular piece of the frame.

Frames are used in Pokemon as well to indicate pokemon type (grass, fire, water, etc), as well as card type (supporter, item, etc).

Frames are great for giving players the ability to scan the battlefield and get a sense of what's happening. One element that frames use is color, which can give the information as to what the type of card is. The frame's color is usually muted and subdued -- it itself is not the star of the show, but should be contrasted with the main art of the card. The illustration should be the star of the show, and the frame but a backdrop.

#### Title
Every card has a name. It would be hard to work through a game with hundreds, or even thousands, of cards otherwise.

The title is the first piece of a card that a player will read. It can give a large impression of the card itself. Is it small? Grand? Clever? Noble? Ambitious?
