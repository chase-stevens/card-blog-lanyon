On this blog, we take a wide view of card games. We look at physical trading card games like Magic the Gathering, digital collectible card games like Hearthstone, and deckbuilding roguelikes like Slay the Spire.

All these games use cards as the primary way of playing the game, but they all have their own variations. Some might be similar games, and others feel vastly different.

Cards in a card game only matter in the context of the game system they are played in.

The most important question to ask is "what is my win condition?" This frames everything you do in the game. Maybe you want to get your opponent's life down to 0 before they do the same to you. Maybe there's a tug of war mechanic where you can pull back from the brink of defeat. You could play a cooperative game where you win and lose along with everyone at the table, instead of playing a zero sum game. Once you understand your objective, and what obstacles are in your way, then you can start to formulate a plan.

Once you figure out your win condition, then you can start to figure out how you're going to achieve that win condition. In Magic, your objective is to get your opponent's life down to 0. 



For card games, another important part of the system is what control you have over your deck of cards. For constructed formats like Standard in Magic the Gathering, you'll have complete control over the cards you play with. In limited formats like draft, you'll come to the event with no cards at all, and pick your cards from a shared pool.

You may also have more than one deck of cards. Some games will split cards that provide resources into their own separate deck away from cards that consume resources to have an effect on the game.

Once you have your cards, you'll want to figure out how they get onto the field and do what you want them to do. Oftentimes, you'll draw an initial hand of cards to start the game. Then on your turn, you'll draw an additional card. This will usually be at the start of your turn, but not always! For example, the four player game Smash Up has players draw cards at the end of their turn instead of at the beginning.

On your turn, you will be able to play cards in exchange for paying a resource. In Magic, this resource would be tapping lands that produce the required mana needed for your cards. In Hearthstone, you spend mana from your mana crystals. In and YuGiOh!, you can play monsters from your hand onto the battlefield without using any visible mechanics, but you do pay a resource here -- you only get to normal summon one monster per turn from your hand.

Okay, so we've brought or built a deck, drawn cards from your deck into your hand, and have paid the necessary costs to play your cards. What now?

In card battlers, you and your opponents will often combat each other. In Magic, players take turns attacking and blocking with their creatures. The attacker chooses which creatures to swing, and the defender chooses how their creatures block the attackers. In Hearthstone, players attack and defend, but it is the attacker who chooses how battle occurs. The attacking minion can target the enemy player, or an enemy minion.

When two creatures battle, the game system will determine the result of the battle. In Magic, if a creature takes damage and lives, all that damage will fall off at the end of the turn, and it's good as new. In Hearthstone, damage persists, so if a minion takes a hit, it will keep that damage for the rest of the game.

Magic and Hearthstone do combat by having the creatures do damage equal to their attack stat to the receiving creature's defense stat. In YuGiOh!, a monster receives damage to either its attack stat or defense stat based on the position its in.
