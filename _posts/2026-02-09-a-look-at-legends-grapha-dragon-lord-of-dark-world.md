Let's take a look at a legendary card from YuGiOh -- Grapha, Dragon Lord of Dark World.

Looking at the card,

We want to mention the statline. This is certainly a beater at 2700 attack. For context, with a clear board, this would take an opponent from full life at 8000 to death in three turns.

And yes, before we get into what makes this card fun, let's acknowledge one of its flaws, a flaw that plagues much of YuGiOh - the templating. The text of the card's abilities is small, wordy, and hard to read. 

I'll share the effect of the monster card here and break it down. But before I do, one note -- "Dark World" is a kindred type of monsters in YuGiOh that cares about discarding your own cards from your hand.

> *You can Special Summon this card (from your Graveyard) by returning 1 face-up "Dark World" monster you control to the hand, except "Grapha, Dragon Lord of Dark World". If this card is discarded to the Graveyard by a card effect: Target 1 card your opponent controls, destroy that target, then, if this card was discarded from your hand to your Graveyard by an opponent's card effect, look at 1 random card in your opponent's hand, then, if it was a monster, you can Special Summon it to your side of the field.*

1. *You can Special Summon this card (from your Graveyard) by returning 1 face-up "Dark World" monster you control to the hand, except "Grapha, Dragon Lord of Dark World".*

This means that if Grapha is in your graveyard, and you have another monster that is part of Grapha's kindred type, you can bounce that monster back to your hand to play Grapha from your graveyard for free.

2. *If this card is discarded to the Graveyard by a card effect: Target 1 card your opponent controls, destroy that target*

Remember what I said about "Dark World" wanting to discard your own cards? This is why -- you can use one of your card effects to discard your Grapha to destroy an opponent's monster. And guess what? Your Grapha is now in the graveyard, where it is eagerly awaiting you to play another "Dark World" monster so you can summon it from the grave.

I will add context that in a Dark World deck, it's fairly easy to discard cards. There are removal spells that have discarding as a cost (Dark World Lightning does this, and allows you to destroy any facedown card), or multiple spells that have you draw a card, then discard a card.

Now, you have most of all the context you need to understand Grapha. For completeness's sake, I'll review the last piece of text on Grapha.

3. *then, if this card was discarded from your hand to your Graveyard by an opponent's card effect, look at 1 random card in your opponent's hand, then, if it was a monster, you can Special Summon it to your side of the field.*

This is a conditional effect which leads to a game of chance. Most of the time, you'll be the one discarding Grapha, so this would not be relevant. However, if you did find yourself in a Dark World matchup, and your opponent played some symmetrical effect that led to you discarding Grapha, you now have a chance to steal a monster card out of their hand and put it on your side of the battlefield.


At the end of the day, we have a removal spell stapled onto a recursive threat that can quickly close out the game. There's certainly some juice here, and for the squeeze, all we need to do is discard it.

It's quite feasible to go second, have an opponent play a monster, then on your turn, play a spell to discard Grapha, destroy opponent's monster with Grapha's effect, then summon a Dark World Monster to then bounce and Special Summon Grapha from the graveyard, then attack a wide open board.


Where does the fun come from?

The core of Grapha, and Dark World's, mechanics come from how they transform discarding, usually a painful cost or punishment in a card game, into a core gameplay mechanic, triggering abilities and graveyard synergies.

Grapha is a boss monster, with multiple abilities rolled into one. A simpler version, like Goldd or Sillva, has a strong attack balue of 2300, and when it is discarded, it is instead special summoned to the battlefield. You exchange power, removal, and recursion in exchange for quicker and more reliable board presense.

Remember, YuGiOh has virtually no resources to manage. Whatever cards you come across in your hand, you can most likely play that turn.

Turning an weakness (discard) into a strength means that you can access powerful abilities and avoid their discard cost, or even make those abilities more powerful.

A similar mechanic in Magic the Gathering, Madness, covers common ground that when you discard a card with a Madness cost, you can instead pay that cost to play that card.



Why does YuGiOh have such cramped text? 

YuGiOh cards are smaller than a Magic the Gathering or a Pokemon card.

Additionaly, when it comes to layout, YuGiOh cards have a much smaller description box for abilities and flavor text.

Compare Grapha to a large creature card in Magic the Gathering.

![Archon of Cruelty](/assets/images/mh2-75-archon-of-cruelty.jpg)

The description box on the Magic card is nearly twice as large as the YuGiOh card. The type line on the Magic card has its own space as well, while the type of the YuGiOh card is fit inside the description box.

As a result, the Magic card can fit the same lines of text, as well as additional flavor text, and have it be more readable than the YuGiOh card.

Grapha is not the worst offender when it comes to cramped text boxes...
